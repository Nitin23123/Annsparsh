5. Case 5: OTP Mismatch & Impersonation / Fraud Attempt

Starring: Nitin (Alert Donor) • Rishi (Suspicious External Claimant) • Aman (Admin)

Scenario: Unauthorized stranger attempts to steal surplus food Theft Blocked by Inverted OTP
Rishi "Namaste, I have a tempo outside. I'm here to collect the free surplus food from AnnSparsh."
Nitin "Which NGO organization sent you, and what is your 4-digit pickup authentication code?"
Rishi "Uh... I think my supervisor said the code is 1234. Or 9999."
Nitin "Entering 1234 on my screen... System displays: 'Invalid OTP. 4 attempts remaining.' Handover denied! You are not authorized. Leave our premises."
Security Defense:
  • API responds with 400 Bad Request: "Invalid OTP".
  • Database increments otp_attempts.
  • After 5 failed attempts, the input locks completely (OTP_LOCKED).
Why Inverted OTP Matters: In ordinary delivery apps, the customer holds the OTP. In AnnSparsh, the NGO holds the OTP and the Donor verifies it. An unauthorized third-party cannot guess or forge the code.

8. Ready Answers for Viva Examiners

Examiner: "Why did you build an inverted OTP rather than standard consumer OTP?"

Answer (by Nitin / Vansh): "In apps like Zomato or Swiggy, the customer receives the OTP because they paid money and confirm delivery. In food donation, food is donated for free. If the donor held the OTP, an unauthorized or rogue driver could take the food and vanish. By having the NGO generate the OTP and giving it to the driver, and requiring the donor to enter it on their own screen, we guarantee that food can only be released to the verified NGO's designated representative."

Examiner: "What happens if a donor repeatedly donates spoiled or unsafe food?"

Answer (by Aman): "We built a Trust & Safety module at /grievances. When an NGO receives spoiled food, they file an incident report with category FOOD_QUALITY. As platform admin, I review the audit log. If transit was prompt but food was spoiled, I mark the report as RESOLVED and trigger REVOKE_VERIFICATION. This immediately revokes their verified badge and prevents them from listing any donations."

Examiner: "How do you handle real-time sync when multiple people are acting simultaneously?"

Answer (by Aditya / Suyash): "We implemented Socket.IO with JWT handshake authentication. Users join personal rooms (user:<id>) and role-based rooms (role:NGO). When Nitin lists food, all NGOs receive donation:available in milliseconds. When Vansh assigns a volunteer, the OTP is strictly emitted to Vansh's personal room. All database state changes are backed by atomic transactions in PostgreSQL."
