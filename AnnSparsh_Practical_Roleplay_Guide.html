2. Case 1: The Golden Flow (Standard Redistribution)

Objective: Prove the core end-to-end redistribution lifecycle under optimal conditions.

Donor Creates Listing (status: AVAILABLE) │ [Socket: donation:available broadcast to role:NGO] ▼ NGO Claims Food (status: PENDING) │ [Socket: request:incoming emitted to user:<donor_id>] ▼ Donor Approves (donation: CLAIMED, request: APPROVED) │ NGO Assigns Volunteer (4-digit OTP generated & visible ONLY to NGO) │ [Physical arrival & verbal OTP quote] ▼ Donor Enters OTP & Verifies (donation: COLLECTED, otp_verified: TRUE)
Stage 1: Surplus Listed on Platform State: AVAILABLE
Donor "Our dinner banquet just concluded. We have 50 untouched portions of vegetable biryani that must be consumed within 4 hours. Let me put this on AnnSparsh."
UI Action: Donor clicks "Donate Food" on /create-donation. Enters Food: Veg Biryani, Qty: 50 plates, Best Before: 4 hours.
DB & Sockets: INSERT INTO donations (status: AVAILABLE). Emits donation:available to role:NGO.
Stage 2: NGO Requests & Donor Approves State: CLAIMED
NGO "A 50-plate donation just appeared in our sector. Requesting immediate pickup for our night shelter beneficiaries."
Donor "Care Relief NGO has submitted a claim. Their verification badge is active. Approved!"
UI Action: NGO clicks "Request Food". Donor sees incoming request toast live, clicks "Approve".
DB & Sockets: Request status becomes APPROVED; donation becomes CLAIMED. Alternate pending requests auto-rejected.
Stage 3: Volunteer Assigned & Inverted OTP Generated OTP Generated
NGO "Assigning volunteer Ramesh Kumar with pickup van MH-12-AB-1234. System generated pickup OTP: 7342. Calling Ramesh now."
Volunteer "Understood. Moving toward Grand Pearl Banquet. Secret OTP is 7342."
UI Action: NGO fills volunteer details in modal. An OTP card appears only on the NGO dashboard with a green toast.
Security Architecture: Server writes 4 digits to requests.otp. Emits strictly to user:<ngo_id> room. The donor cannot see this OTP.
Stage 4: Doorstep Handoff & OTP Verification State: COLLECTED
Volunteer "Namaste, I am Ramesh from Care Relief. Here to collect the 50 plates of Biryani. My pickup OTP is 7342."
Donor "Checking food containers. Looks fresh and packaged properly. Entering 7342 into my AnnSparsh screen... Verified!"
UI Action: Donor enters 7342 into the verification input and presses "Confirm OTP".
DB & Sockets: requests.otp_verified = TRUE, donation status: COLLECTED. Emits pickup:collected to both sides simultaneously.

3. Case 2: Order Delivered, But Grievance Filed (Post-Delivery Food Quality Dispute)

Objective: Answer the project guide's critical question: "What happens when an order is marked delivered, but the food is spoiled or compromised upon arrival at the shelter?"

Pickup Completed via OTP (status: COLLECTED) │ [Containers transported to Community Shelter] ▼ Food Inspected at Shelter (Containers opened -> sour smell / spoilage detected) │ [NGO prevents food distribution to beneficiaries] ▼ NGO Files Grievance on Portal (/grievances -> Ticket REP-00042) │ [Socket: report:created emitted to role:ADMIN] ▼ Admin Reviews Audit Log (best_before window vs pickup timestamp) │ [Admin investigates & applies sanction] ▼ Admin Resolves Dispute (Donor issued warning or Account Revocation via REVOKE_VERIFICATION)
Stage 1: The Incident (Post-Pickup Discovery) Food Safety Hazard
Volunteer "Handover was completed smoothly with OTP 7342 and containers arrived at the shelter 35 minutes later."
NGO Coordinator "Stop! Do not distribute these containers. As soon as we unsealed the lids, the biryani is curdled, sour, and smelling rancid. The donor had left this near hot kitchen boilers before pickup."
NGO Coordinator "Serving this could cause mass food poisoning. We must dispose of this batch immediately and file a formal grievance on AnnSparsh with photo evidence."
Key Principle: OTP verification certifies that custody was transferred, but it does NOT absolve the donor of food safety liability under FSSAI and AnnSparsh terms.
Stage 2: NGO Submits Grievance Report Ticket: REP-00042
NGO Coordinator "Opening Trust & Safety portal. Filing report against Donor for Donation #108. Category: Food Quality / Safety. Severity: HIGH."
UI Action on /grievances:
  • Reported Role: Food Donor
  • Category: Food Quality / Safety
  • Severity: HIGH
  • Donation ID: 108
  • Description: "50 servings of biryani spoiled prior to collection. Severe sour odor upon opening containers at shelter. Safe disposal conducted under supervisor."
Backend Execution:
  • API: POST /api/reports
  • Database writes row in reports table with status = 'PENDING'.
  • Generates ticket ID REP-00042.
  • Socket broadcasts report:created to all connected Admins.
Stage 3: Admin Review & Investigation State: INVESTIGATING
Admin "Incoming High-Severity Report REP-00042 on Donation #108. Let's inspect the timeline and audit logs."
Admin Audit Checklist:
  1. Listing Time: 11:30 PM (Donor claimed 4 hours freshness).
  2. Collection Time: 12:15 AM (Transit took only 45 minutes).
  3. Deduction: The food could not spoil within 45 minutes in winter temperatures unless it was already spoiled at 11:30 PM. The donor violated the hygiene declaration.
Stage 4: Administrative Resolution & Sanctions State: RESOLVED
Admin "Marking ticket RESOLVED. Adding admin note: 'Cold-chain audit shows food spoiled prior to pickup. Formal strike issued to donor.' Executing account suspension."
UI Action: Admin selects "REVOKE_VERIFICATION" from action menu and submits resolution notes.
Database Update: UPDATE users SET is_verified = FALSE, verification_status = 'REJECTED', rejection_reason = 'Account suspended following grievance review (Report #42)' WHERE id = <donor_id>;
The donor cannot create new listings until re-verified.

4. Case 3: Doorstep Mismatch (Pre-OTP Inspection Rejection)

Objective: Demonstrate why physical inspection MUST precede OTP entry.

Scenario: Listed 50 meals, but only 4 are presented Pre-Handoff Discrepancy
Volunteer "Hello, I am here for the 50 meals of Dal & Rice."
Donor "Sorry, our staff took most of it home. We only have 4 small packets left. Please give me your OTP so I can close the order."
Volunteer "No. AnnSparsh policy strictly forbids sharing the OTP if the physical quantity does not match the listing. We cannot mobilize a transport van for 4 packets. I am refusing pickup."
Outcome:
  • OTP is never entered by the donor.
  • NGO clicks "Withdraw / Cancel Request" on /ngo-dashboard.
  • NGO files report: Category FRAUD & MISREPRESENTATION.
Database Status:
  • Donation never transitions to COLLECTED.
  • otp_verified remains FALSE.
  • Donor cannot falsely inflate CSR impact or meal statistics.

6. Case 5: OTP Mismatch & Impersonation / Fraud Attempt

Objective: Prove how the inverted OTP prevents food theft and impersonation.

Scenario: Unauthorized stranger attempts to claim food Security Threat Blocked
Stranger "Hello, I am here with a bike to collect the free surplus food."
Donor "Which NGO are you registered with, and what is your 4-digit pickup code?"
Stranger "Uh... I think the code is 1234. Or maybe 9999."
Donor "Entering 1234... System says: 'Invalid OTP'. Handover denied. Please leave the premises."
Security Safeguard:
  • API returns 400 Bad Request: "Invalid OTP".
  • otp_attempts incremented in DB.
  • After 3 failed attempts, donor UI enforces a 5-minute lockout to block brute-force guessing.
Why Inverted OTP Matters: In standard delivery apps (Uber, Swiggy), the customer gets the OTP. In AnnSparsh, the NGO holds the OTP and the Donor verifies it. This guarantees that only the NGO's dispatched representative can authenticate the pickup.

7. Case 6: High-Demand Race Condition (Multiple Claims)

Objective: Concurrency safety when several NGOs claim the same surplus listing.

Timeline 10:00:00 AM — Hotel lists 100 meals. Status: AVAILABLE
NGO A (Feed Pune) 10:01:15 AM — Clicks "Request Food" (Distance: 3 km away).
NGO B (Seva Trust) 10:01:42 AM — Clicks "Request Food" (Distance: 12 km away).
Donor 10:02:10 AM — Reviews both incoming requests on /donor-dashboard. Selects NGO A due to closer proximity and faster turnaround time. Clicks "Approve".
Database Atomic Resolution: When the donor approves NGO A:
  1. requests.status for NGO A updates to APPROVED.
  2. donations.status updates to CLAIMED.
  3. All other requests for the same donation_id are automatically updated to REJECTED in a single transaction.
  4. NGO B's dashboard receives Socket event request:resolved displaying a polite toast: "This donation has been allocated to another organization."

9. Viva Anticipated Questions & Strong Model Answers

Q1: "Why did you invert the OTP flow instead of standard delivery app flows?"

Model Answer: "In commercial delivery (e.g. Swiggy/Zomato), the customer pays money and receives the OTP to confirm receipt from the driver. In surplus food redistribution, food is donated for free. If the donor held the OTP, an irresponsible driver could pick up food and disappear without delivering. By giving the OTP to the verified NGO and having the donor enter it, we guarantee that only an authorized NGO agent can authenticate the transaction at the donor's doorstep."

Q2: "What prevents a donor from donating rotten food to get tax/CSR certificates?"

Model Answer: "Two layers of defense: First, the on-site volunteer physical inspection before exchanging the OTP. Second, our Trust & Safety Grievance Module (/grievances). If spoiled food is detected post-delivery, the NGO files an incident report (FOOD_QUALITY). Admins audit the timestamp log, and if donor negligence is found, the admin invokes REVOKE_VERIFICATION which suspends their account and voids all CSR audit logs."

Q3: "How does the platform handle real-time sync across all these users?"

Model Answer: "We implemented a dual-room Socket.IO architecture. When a user connects, their socket authenticates via JWT and joins a private room user:<id> and a role room like role:NGO. Broadcasts like new donations go to role:NGO, while private sensitive events like OTP generation target strictly user:<ngo_id>. This prevents information leakage while enabling sub-second multi-device UI synchronization."
